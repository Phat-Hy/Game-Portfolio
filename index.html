81 Days and Nights

Enemy AI & gameplay systems · AnomalyVN Studio · Unity + C#

A historical single-player FPS set in the 1972 Battle of Quang Tri, with a four-mission campaign and an endless Survival mode.

What I built

  • NavMesh-driven enemy AI — three behaviour modes (Wander / Ambush / Sniper), perception by line-of-sight raycast.
  • A cover system with positional claims, so squadmates never fight over the same wall.
  • Peek and flanking behaviours, with engagement distances spread across the squad.
  • Editor tooling: custom inspectors, a CLI verification harness, an automated API integration runner.

Why it mattered

Twelve soldiers that each pick the nearest wall read as one dumb soldier, twelve times. The work was making a group look coordinated while staying cheap enough to run.

Case studies

Two problems worth explaining, because the code was never the hard part.

01

Making a squad stop clumping

Problem

Every agent evaluated cover in isolation, so a squad of twelve would all claim the best wall, stack into one silhouette, and die together.

Approach

Cover points are claimed before movement, so a claimed point drops out of what the next agent can evaluate. Engagement distance is spread across the squad, and targets are distributed instead of first-come.

Result

Enemies fan into readable positions rather than a pile. You can tell at a glance who is suppressing and who is repositioning.

02

Balancing difficulty without a designer

Problem

Enemy difficulty is a bag of numbers: health, accuracy, how often they push versus take cover. Tuning that by hand, per player, is not a one-person job.

Approach

The client reports what actually happened in each fight. A .NET service scores every enemy configuration with a fitness function, then breeds the next generation — elitist selection, 60/40 blending crossover, ±5% mutation, gated until each candidate has seen enough real fights.

Result

Difficulty evolves per player from evidence instead of guesswork, and parameters update in a live build without shipping a patch.
